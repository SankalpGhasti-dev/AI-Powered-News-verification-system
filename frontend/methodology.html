Verification methodology & scoring

VeritasLens operates under transparent, automated evidence evaluation principles: decomposing articles into testable claims, gathering external corroboration via GNews and Google Fact Check Tools API, and calculating mathematical credibility scores.

1. Core Verification Principles

1

Atomic claim decomposition

Articles are deconstructed into standalone, falsifiable propositions using Google Gemini, isolating testable claims from subjective rhetoric.

2

Multi-source wire corroboration

Claims are cross-referenced across accredited news reporting via GNews API and established fact-checking records via Google Fact Check Tools API.

3

Mathematical scoring transparency

Credibility scores (0–100) are computed through a reproducible formula based on verdict distribution and evidence coverage.

4

Explicit evidentiary boundaries

Every dossier clearly documents evidence gaps, paywall exclusions, and retrieval limitations so users know the bounds of the evaluation.

2. Standard Verification Verdicts

The backend engine assigns each claim and overarching investigation one of four explicit verdicts based strictly on the retrieved records:

SUPPORTED Corroborated by Retrieved Evidence

Substantive, reputable evidence from accredited news wire sources or audited fact-checking databases independently corroborates the factual assertion.

CONTRADICTED Disproven or Refuted by Evidence

Documented data, official registry releases, or authoritative investigative reporting directly refute or contradict the assertion.

MIXED Partially Supported or Misleading Context

The assertion contains verified elements mixed with misleading, exaggerated, unverified, or outdated context that distorts the full picture.

INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE Inconclusive Public Records

No credible corroborating or refuting reporting was found in available news archives. The system maintains an evidentiary neutral baseline (score: 50.0) rather than assuming falsehood without positive evidence.

3. Credibility Scoring Formula

The VeritasLens Credibility Score is a calibrated mathematical metric from 0 to 100 reflecting evidence support rather than philosophical certainty:

Score = 50.0 + (VerdictBalance × 45.0 × CoverageFactor)

Where the mathematical components are defined as:

  • Verdict Balance: Computed as (SupportedClaims - ContradictedClaims) / TotalClaims, producing a value between -1.0 and +1.0.
  • Coverage Factor: Measures how many distinct evidence sources were retrieved relative to the number of claims: min(1.0, EvidenceCount / (max(1, TotalClaims) × 2)).
  • Neutral Baseline: If no corroborating or contradicting evidence is retrieved, the score defaults to 50.0, clearly distinguishing lack of data from confirmed refutation.

4. Prototype Boundaries & Disclosures

To maintain full research transparency, users should understand the operational boundaries of this system:

  • Evidence Support vs. Truth: The score measures empirical corroboration found across indexed public news wires and fact-checking databases. An insufficient evidence score does not imply that an event did not happen—merely that documented reporting was not located.
  • AI Decomposition Role: Google Gemini is utilized to parse text into claims and synthesize findings. Gemini does not act as an independent omniscient arbiter of truth; its outputs are strictly constrained by the evidence retrieved.
  • Source Ingestion Limits: Paywalled content, subscription-only wire terminals, regional language broadcasts, and breaking events younger than 24 hours may not be fully indexed by external search APIs.
  • Local Browser Storage: Investigation history is preserved locally in your browser's localStorage. No user accounts, tracking cookies, or persistent database records are maintained on the server.
